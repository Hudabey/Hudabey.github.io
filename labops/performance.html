08 · Performance at scale

What happens at millions of rows?

run_metrics is the growth table: at a realistic 200 runs/week × 40 metrics each, the lab writes ~8,000 rows/week — about 4.2 million rows in year ten. Performance is a design property, not an afterthought.

Why the green line stays flat

B+-tree depth grows logarithmically (fanout ~200): the composite index on (run_id, metric_name) resolves a point probe in 3–4 page reads even at 107 rows. The red line is why EXPLAIN-driven review is policy: any new query touching run_metrics must show an index range scan before it ships.

Mitigations, in order

1 — Covering indexes for the hot aggregates.

2 — RANGE partitioning on run_id — the table intentionally carries no timestamp (3NF: the run's date lives on experiment_runs), and run_id is monotonically increasing, so it is a faithful proxy for time.

3 — Summary tables refreshed by trigger or scheduled job.

The audit table is append-only

run_audit carries no secondary indexes and — deliberately — no FK on run_id: an audit trail must survive the deletion of the run it describes. A correctness decision that happens to also be the fast one.
